# Listening and Active Communication

## 1. Active Listening

**Meaning:** Carefully listening to someone, understanding what they say, and paying attention instead of just hearing their words.

### Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

1. **Avoid distractions** – Keep your phone aside and avoid unnecessary interruptions.
2. **Focus on the speaker** – Pay attention to the person and the topic being discussed.
3. **Don't interrupt** – Let the other person complete their thoughts.
4. **Wait and respond** – Think about what was said before giving your response.
5. **Use door openers** – Encourage the speaker using phrases like:
   - Tell me more.
   - Go ahead.
   - I'm listening.
   - That's interesting.
6. **Use positive body language** – Maintain appropriate eye contact, nod and show interest.
7. **Take notes** – Write down important points whenever required.
8. **Paraphrase** – Repeat the main idea in your own words to confirm your understanding.

**Remember:** Focus → Listen → Encourage → Don't interrupt → Take notes → Paraphrase.

## 2. Reflective Listening

**Meaning:** Listening carefully and repeating the meaning of what someone said in your own words to show that you understand them.

Reflective Listening is a subset of Active Listening. It focuses more on understanding the speaker's thoughts, emotions and intentions.

### Key techniques

- **Mirroring:** Repeat important words or phrases to show that you are listening.
- **Paraphrasing:** Explain what the speaker said using your own words.
- **Reflecting emotions:** Recognise and acknowledge the speaker's feelings.
- **Clarifying:** Ask questions to make sure you understood correctly.
- **Summarising:** Briefly repeat the important points of the conversation.
- **Empathy:** Try to understand the situation from the speaker's point of view without judging them.

### Reflective Listening in Open-Ended Dialogue

- Skilled communicators repeat or rephrase others' words to show understanding, especially during emotional conversations.
- It helps acknowledge feelings, show empathy and clarify doubts.
- Chatbots can also use this technique by rephrasing users' questions before responding.

**Remember:** Echo → Rephrase → Show understanding → Respond with empathy.

### Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

1. Allow the speaker to finish without interrupting.
2. Remember the important facts and points shared by the speaker.
3. Take notes whenever necessary.
4. Repeat or summarise the main points to confirm understanding.
5. Keep an open mind, even when you disagree with someone's opinion.
6. Avoid judging or reacting negatively to different opinions.
7. Show genuine interest in the conversation.

### Application in Business / Software Development

In business meetings and technical discussions:

1. Listen carefully to the discussion.
2. Take notes about important points and requirements.
3. Share the meeting notes with all stakeholders.
4. Ask others to verify and correct any mistakes.
5. Make sure everyone agrees on the discussed points.
6. Refer to the notes during implementation to avoid missing important details.

**Remember:** Listen → Take notes → Share → Verify → Ensure everyone is on the same page.

## 3. Reflection

### Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

Some common obstacles I experience are:

1. **Distractions:** Notifications, mobile phones and surrounding noise.
2. **Overthinking:** Thinking about my response instead of listening to the speaker.
3. **Assumptions:** Assuming I already know what the other person is going to say.
4. **Interruptions:** Sometimes responding before the other person finishes.
5. **Lack of interest:** Losing focus when the topic is not interesting.
6. **Emotional reactions:** Getting distracted by my emotions when I disagree with someone.

### Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

1. Avoid distractions and give the speaker my full attention.
2. Listen to understand rather than listening just to respond.
3. Avoid interrupting and allow others to complete their thoughts.
4. Keep an open mind and avoid making assumptions.
5. Ask questions whenever something is unclear.
6. Take notes during important discussions.
7. Practise paraphrasing to confirm my understanding.
8. Be patient and try to understand other people's perspectives.

## 4. Types of Communication

There are four common communication styles.

### 1. Passive Communication

**Meaning:** Not expressing our thoughts, feelings or opinions openly because we don't want to upset others or create conflict.

**Example:**
- Agreeing with something even when I disagree.
- Avoiding saying NO even when I am uncomfortable.
- Keeping my opinions to myself.

### 2. Aggressive Communication

**Meaning:** Expressing our thoughts and opinions without respecting other people's feelings or opinions.

**Example:**
- Raising my voice during an argument.
- Blaming others for mistakes.
- Forcing others to accept my opinion.
- Not allowing others to speak.

### 3. Passive-Aggressive Communication

**Meaning:** Not expressing anger or disagreement directly, but showing it indirectly through sarcasm, taunts, gossip or silent treatment.

**Example:**
- Giving sarcastic replies instead of explaining the actual problem.
- Ignoring someone intentionally.
- Making indirect comments when I am unhappy.
- Complaining about someone to others instead of talking to them directly.

### 4. Assertive Communication

**Meaning:** Expressing our thoughts, feelings and opinions clearly and confidently while respecting other people's opinions and feelings.

**Example:**
- Politely disagreeing with someone's opinion.
- Saying NO when necessary.
- Expressing concerns without blaming others.
- Sharing ideas confidently during meetings.

**Remember:**

| Style | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Passive | I don't express myself. |
| Aggressive | Only my opinion matters. |
| Passive-Aggressive | I express my anger indirectly. |
| Assertive | I express myself while respecting others. |

## 5. Personal Reflection

### Question 5: When do you switch to Passive Communication Style in your day-to-day life?

- When talking to seniors or people in authority.
- When I am unsure whether my opinion is correct.
- When I want to avoid unnecessary arguments.
- When I don't want to disappoint someone.

### Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive Communication Style in your day-to-day life?

- When someone repeatedly ignores my suggestions.
- When I get frustrated because of repeated mistakes.
- When someone blames me for something that is not my fault.
- When I feel that someone is not respecting my time or effort.

### Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive Communication Style in your day-to-day life?

- When I am upset but don't want to discuss the issue directly.
- When I use sarcasm instead of expressing what is bothering me.
- When I intentionally avoid responding to someone.
- When I make indirect comments instead of communicating my concerns openly.

## 6. Assertive Communication

### Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?

Assertive communication means expressing my feelings, opinions and needs clearly while respecting other people.

### Steps I can apply in my daily life

1. **Understand my feelings:** Identify what I am actually feeling before responding.
2. **Express my needs:** Clearly explain what I need instead of expecting others to understand automatically.
3. **Use "I" statements:** Explain how I feel without blaming others.
   - Instead of: "You never listen to me."
   - Say: "I feel ignored when my suggestions are not considered."
4. **Start with small situations:** Practise expressing my opinions with friends and colleagues.
5. **Maintain positive body language:** Use a calm tone, appropriate eye contact and confident posture.
6. **Respect other opinions:** Understand that others may have different perspectives.
7. **Speak up early:** Address problems before they become bigger issues.
8. **Stay calm:** Avoid reacting immediately when I am angry or frustrated.

### Practical Example

**Situation:** A colleague keeps interrupting me during a meeting.

- Passive: Stay silent and allow them to continue.
- Aggressive: "Stop interrupting me! You never let anyone speak."
- Passive-Aggressive: "Some people think they know everything."
- Assertive: "I'd like to finish my point. I'll be happy to hear your thoughts once I'm done."

### My Key Takeaway

I should not stay silent just to avoid conflict, and I should not force others to accept my opinions.

I should communicate openly, confidently and respectfully.

**Remember:** Be clear → Be confident → Be respectful → Listen to others.
