
# Tiny Habits

## Main Idea

Big changes start with small actions.

Instead of depending on motivation or willpower, make a new habit so small and easy that you can do it every day.

## 1. B = MAP Model

A behaviour happens when three things come together:

- **M – Motivation:** You want to do it.
- **A – Ability:** You can do it easily.
- **P – Prompt:** Something reminds you to do it.

If one of these is missing, the behaviour may not happen.

## 2. Start Small
**Make habits very small.** Start with an action that takes only a few seconds.

Don't start with a big goal. Start with a tiny action.

Examples:
- Read one sentence.
- Do one push-up.
- Floss one tooth.
- Take one deep breath.

Small actions require less effort and are easier to repeat.

## 3. Use an Existing Habit as a Trigger
**Use an anchor.** Connect the new habit to an existing daily routine.

Connect your new habit to something you already do every day.

Formula:

After I [existing habit], I will [tiny habit].

Example:

After I brush my teeth, I will do one push-up.

## 4. Celebrate Immediately
**Celebrate immediately.** Feel good after completing the tiny action.

After completing your tiny habit, create a positive feeling.

Examples:
- Smile.
- Say "Yes!"
- Give yourself a small fist pump.

Feeling good after completing an action helps encourage the habit.

## 5. Don't Depend on Motivation

Motivation changes every day.

Instead of waiting to feel motivated, make the habit easy enough to do even when you are tired or busy.

## 6. Let the Habit Grow Naturally

**Let habits grow naturally.** Small actions can gradually become bigger habits.

Start with a tiny version and allow it to grow over time.

Example:

One push-up → Five push-ups → A regular workout.

You don't need to force yourself to do more every day.

## 7. Don't Blame Yourself
**You are not the problem; your habit design may be.** Don't blame yourself when a habit fails.
  
If a habit fails, don't think you are lazy or weak.

Instead, ask:
- Is the habit too difficult?
- Is the prompt missing?
- Can I make it smaller?

Change the design of the habit instead of blaming yourself.

## Summary

**Make it tiny, attach it to an existing routine, and celebrate your success.**

Small actions repeated consistently can lead to meaningful changes.

Don't try harder. Make the habit easier to do.

# 1% Better Every Day

## Main Idea
Small improvements repeated consistently can lead to big results over time. Good habits compound like interest.

## Key Points

- **Small improvements matter:** British Cycling improved many small things and achieved great success.
- **Clarity over motivation:** Decide exactly when, where, and how you will do a habit.
- **Design your environment:** Make good habits visible and bad habits difficult to access.
- **Start small:** Use the Two-Minute Rule to make starting easy.
- **Focus on repetition:** Practice consistently instead of waiting for perfect results.
- **Track your progress:** Seeing your progress motivates you to continue.
- **Build your identity:** Every action is a vote for the person you want to become.

## Summary:
You don't need to change everything overnight. Improve a little every day, and let consistency create meaningful change.

# Atomic Habits

## Main Idea
Small habits, repeated consistently, lead to remarkable results over time.

## Key Points

- **1% Better Every Day:** Small improvements compound into big changes.
- **Identity-Based Habits:** Focus on becoming the person you want to be, not just achieving a goal.
- **Four Laws of Behaviour Change:**
  1. Make it obvious – Keep cues visible.
  2. Make it attractive – Make the habit appealing.
  3. Make it easy – Reduce effort and start small.
  4. Make it satisfying – Reward yourself after completing it.
- **Break Bad Habits:** Reverse the four laws by making them invisible, unattractive, difficult, and unsatisfying.
- **Environment Matters:** Design your surroundings to support good habits.

## Summary:


You don't rise to the level of your goals; you fall to the level of your systems.
